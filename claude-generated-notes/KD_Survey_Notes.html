0. The big picture

What KD is. A lightweight student is trained with extra supervision from a bigger teacher. Idea first proposed by Buciluǎ et al. (2006), popularised by Hinton (2015) as training the student on the teacher's output distribution (soft labels). Unlike pruning, quantization, or low-rank factorization, KD does not modify the network's layers or parameters; it changes only how the student is trained.

0.5 Math primer: the KD objective in one place

Notation. x = input, y = ground-truth label, K = number of classes, T / S = teacher / student, z_t, z_s = logits, F_t, F_s = intermediate features, τ = temperature, σ = softmax.

Temperature softmax and soft labels (survey Eq. 2)

p_i(z; τ) = exp(z_i / τ) / Σ_j exp(z_j / τ) τ = 1 → ordinary softmax; τ → ∞ → uniform; τ → 0 → one-hot (argmax)

Logit distillation loss (survey Eq. 3) and the usual full objective standard form

KL(p_t ‖ p_s) = Σ_i p_t,i · log( p_t,i / p_s,i ) L_total = (1 − λ) · CE( y , p(z_s; 1) ) + λ · τ² · KL( p(z_t; τ) ‖ p(z_s; τ) ) └── hard-label (task) term ──┘ └──── distillation term (Eq. 3) ────┘

The survey's Eq. (3) is only the second (distillation) term. The τ² factor keeps gradient magnitudes comparable when τ changes, because:

∂L_KD / ∂z_s,i = (1/τ) · ( p_s,i(τ) − p_t,i(τ) ) for large τ and zero-mean logits: ≈ 1/(K·τ²) · ( z_s,i − z_t,i ) → at high temperature KD ≈ directly regressing the student's logits onto the teacher's (logit matching)

Link to label smoothing (the survey notes KD "can be likened to label smoothing")

label smoothing target: q_i = (1 − ε)·1[i = y] + ε / K (uniform "teacher") KD target: q_i = p_i(z_t; τ) (data-dependent "teacher")

General multi-source objective standard form

L = L_task + α · L_ld (Eq. 1/3) + β · L_fd (Eq. 4) + γ · L_SD (Eq. 5) Eq. 1: L_ld(Z_T, Z_S) = ℓ_logit(Z_T, Z_S) Eq. 4: L_fd = ℓ_feature( Φ_t(F_t), Φ_s(F_s) ) Eq. 5: L_SD = ℓ_similarity( φ(F_t^i, F_t^j), φ(F_s^i, F_s^j) )

Scheme-dependent optimisation standard form

Offline : θ_T frozen; min_θS L(θ_S ; θ_T) Online : both trained together; min_{θS,θT} L_S + L_T (mutual learning) L_S = CE(y,p_s) + KL(p_t ‖ p_s) ; L_T = CE(y,p_t) + KL(p_s ‖ p_t) Self-distillation : θ_T = θ_S from an earlier epoch / branch / EMA
Reading the survey's seven equations (1) generic logit loss · (2) temperature softmax · (3) KL on soft labels · (4) generic feature loss with transforms Φ · (5) generic similarity loss with relation function φ · (6) adversarially robust distillation (ARD) · (7) contrastive distillation (InfoNCE-style). Everything else in the survey is a choice of ℓ, Φ, φ, which layers, which samples, and when.

1. Introduction & motivation

  • Problem. DNNs, transformers, VLMs and LLMs are heavily over-parameterized (Kaplan 2020; Fischer 2024; LLM weight matrices are shown to be low-rank, Hu 2021 / LoRA) and expensive in runtime and memory, which blocks edge-device deployment (mobile phones).
  • Alternatives to KD: efficient blocks (MobileNet, ShuffleNet, BiSeNet), pruning (remove unneeded layers/params), quantization, low-rank factorization.
  • Other purposes of KD beyond compression:
    • Knowledge transfer from a source task to a target task with insufficient labels.
    • Data-free KD when privacy is a concern: synthesize data so sensitive data need not be stored.
  • Why a new survey? Prior surveys: Gou et al. 2021 (sources/algorithms/schemes; most prominent), Wang & Yoon 2021 (vision teacher-student compression), Alkhulaifi 2021 (new size-vs-accuracy metric), Yang 2023c (by knowledge source), Hu 2023a (optimization objectives), Moslemi 2024 (update + brief VLM / limited-data discussion). Gaps the authors claim:
    1. Recent feature-based methods (now dominating SOTA) are under-covered; adaptive and contrastive distillation barely discussed.
    2. Foundation models and LLMs (as teachers/targets) are mostly ignored (e.g. Gou 2021).
    3. 3D inputs (point clouds) are not reviewed at all.

2. Sources of distillation

Three categories (Fig. 3): logits (last-layer output), features (intermediate activations / neurons / feature maps), similarities (relationships among channels, instances, classes, regions). Tables 2 and 3 in the paper summarize them.

2.1 Logit-based distillation

Student mimics the teacher's final predictions, which carry "dark knowledge" (Hinton). What "logits" mean depends on task: class scores (classification), predicted coordinates (detection), heatmaps (pose estimation).

L_ld(Z_T, Z_S) = ℓ_logit(Z_T, Z_S) (1) p(Z_i, τ) = exp(Z_i/τ) / Σ_i exp(Z_i/τ) (2) softmax with temperature τ L_ld = KL( p(Z_t, τ) , p(Z_s, τ) ) (3)

τ controls softening: higher τ → flatter distribution exposing inter-class similarity. First KD papers: Hinton 2015; Ba & Caruana 2014.

Math behind the logit families standard form

Decoupled KD (DKD; Zhao 2022a) — split KL into target-class and non-target-class parts b = [p_target, 1 − p_target] (binary target / non-target probability) p̂ = non-target probabilities renormalised to sum to 1 KL(p_t ‖ p_s) = KL(b_t ‖ b_s) + (1 − p_t,target) · KL(p̂_t ‖ p̂_s) └── TCKD ──┘ └──────────── NCKD ─────────────┘ L_DKD = α · TCKD + β · NCKD (NCKD is suppressed in vanilla KD by the factor (1 − p_t,target) when the teacher is confident → decoupling lets β be set freely) Logit standardisation (Sun 2024a) — Z-score before softmax, temperature = std Z(z) = ( z − mean(z) ) / std(z) L = τ² · KL( σ(Z(z_t)/τ) ‖ σ(Z(z_s)/τ) ) (removes the need for teacher/student logit ranges to match) NormKD (Chi 2023) — sample-wise temperature τ_x = f( statistics of the sample's logit distribution ) instead of one constant τ Spherical KD (Guo 2020a) — match direction only L = ℓ( z_t/‖z_t‖ , z_s/‖z_s‖ ) (confidence magnitude is discarded)

Why "decoupling" matters (survey §2.1): methods by Zhao 2022a, Li 2024a, Ding 2024 separate the target-class and non-target-class distributions; Cui 2024 (DKL) re-writes KL into a weighted MSE plus a cross-entropy that carries the soft labels; Wei 2024b (SDD) distils logits at multiple spatial scales; Liu 2024d (BPKD) weights edge vs. body pixels differently for segmentation.

Take-aways (paper's summary) Logit KD is simple and acts like label smoothing / regularization (Kim & Kim 2017; Müller 2019), preventing overfitting. Limits: depends on predefined classes → only supervised learning; gives no insight into the teacher's intermediate layers; hard to learn from only final outputs when architectures differ → motivates feature and similarity distillation.

2.2 Feature-based distillation

Intermediate representations give "step-by-step" information leading to the prediction; especially valuable when the model output is a representation (not logits). First: FitNet / hints (Romero 2014) — match intermediate features.

L_fd = ℓ_feature( Φ_t(F_t), Φ_s(F_s) ) (4) Φ = transformation that matches spatial size / channel count; ℓ_feature = similarity function

Chronology of ideas

  • Basic matching/statistics: AT (Zagoruyko & Komodakis 2016): attention transfer — aggregate channels into attention maps, L2 between them. NST (Huang & Wang 2017): match neuron-selectivity distributions with Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD). AB (Heo 2019b): transfer activation boundaries of hidden neurons. FT (Kim 2018): transfer "factors" via a paraphraser auto-encoder. Heo 2019a (OFD): "overhaul" — distill pre-activation features (margin ReLU, partial L2). CWD (Shu 2021): channel-wise distillation — softmax over channels, match distributions via KL.
  • Cross-layer: SimKD (Chen 2022a): reuses teacher classifier. Knowledge Review (Chen 2021d): each student layer matches the corresponding teacher layer and all previous ones. SemCKD (Chen 2021a): every student feature matches all teacher features with learned weights (FitNet = special case with weight 1 for the matched pair).
  • Focus on the transformation Φ: Heo 2019b auto-encoder; MGD (Yang 2022f): randomly mask student feature pixels, then 2 conv layers must regenerate teacher features; MLP (Liu 2023c): masking is unnecessary — a simple MLP projector suffices; DiffKD (Huang 2023b): train a diffusion model on teacher features and use it to denoise student features (student = noisy teacher); AttnFD (Mansourian 2024): CBAM channel+spatial attention applied to both teacher and student features, then MSE on refined features.
  • Other recent: LAD (Liu 2024g): FitNet loss decomposed into magnitude + angular terms. FAKD (Yuan 2024a): match teacher features to augmented student features. FreeKD (Zhang 2024i): frequency-domain distillation. Liu 2023a (Norm): N-to-one matching. Passban 2021: fuse all teacher features (matching layer-to-layer causes some teacher layers to be ignored).

Math of the main feature-distillation losses standard form

FitNet (hint + regressor r): L = ‖ F_t − r(F_s) ‖²₂ r = 1×1 conv / linear Attention Transfer (AT): Q(F) = Σ_c |F_c|^p (sum over channels → H×W map) L_AT = Σ_j ‖ Q_s^j/‖Q_s^j‖₂ − Q_t^j/‖Q_t^j‖₂ ‖_p Neuron Selectivity Transfer (NST): L = MMD²(F_t, F_s) = ‖ (1/N) Σ_i φ(f_t^i) − (1/M) Σ_j φ(f_s^j) ‖² (matches the distribution of neuron activation patterns; φ = kernel feature map) Channel-wise distillation (CWD): φ(y_c)_i = exp(y_c,i / T) / Σ_i exp(y_c,i / T) (spatial softmax per channel c) L = (T² / C) · Σ_c KL( φ(y^T_c) ‖ φ(y^S_c) ) Overhaul (OFD, Heo 2019a): teacher pre-ReLU feature through margin ReLU σ_m(x) = max(x, m), m = E[x | x < 0] partial L2: d_p(T,S) = Σ_i 0 if S_i ≤ T_i and T_i ≤ 0 (T_i − S_i)² otherwise Masked generative (MGD): M_{i,j} ~ Bernoulli(1−λ) (random pixel mask) L = Σ ‖ F_t − G( M ⊙ F_s ) ‖²₂ (G = two conv layers regenerating the teacher map) Knowledge Review: L = Σ_i D( U( fuse( F_s^i … F_s^n ) ) , F_t^i ) (student layer i is matched to the teacher's layer i using the fused deeper-to-shallower student features) SemCKD (all-to-all, learned weights): L = Σ_{i,j} α_{i,j} · ‖ F_t^j − r_j(F_s^i) ‖² (FitNet = α_{i,j} = 1 for the matched pair, 0 else) AttnFD (CBAM): L = ‖ CBAM(F_t) − CBAM(F_s) ‖²₂ (channel attention then spatial attention, then MSE) LAD (magnitude + angle): L = ‖ ‖F_t‖ − ‖F_s‖ ‖ + (1 − cos∠(F_t, F_s)) (decomposition of the FitNet L2 loss) DiffKD (denoising): treat F_s ≈ F_t + noise; a diffusion model trained on F_t outputs F̂_s = Denoise(F_s) L = ℓ( F̂_s , F_t ) (+ KL on the logits obtained from the denoised feature)

Further feature-method details from the survey: Kim 2018 (FT) uses an auto-encoder "paraphraser" so the student's translator can mimic teacher "factors"; Heo 2019b (AB) binarises activations so only the activation boundary (sign pattern) is transferred; Srinivas & Fleuret 2018 match the Jacobian of the feature w.r.t. the input; Xu 2020b (FN) L2-normalises both features and uses cross-entropy; He 2019 (adapted feature) uses an auto-encoder on the teacher and a 3×3 conv on the student; Yuan 2024a (FAKD) transfers the activation boundary with KL after augmenting student features; Liu 2023a ("Norm") performs N-to-one teacher-to-student layer matching; Passban 2021 argues one-to-one matching ignores some teacher layers and fuses all teacher features instead.

Sensitivity issue (survey): feature KD results depend on (i) which layers are used, (ii) how teacher/student layers are aligned, and (iii) the transformation function, so comparing methods across papers is not straightforward.

Take-aways Feature KD is more generalizable (richer knowledge) and most SOTA methods are feature-based. Hard parts: choosing which layers, aligning teacher/student layers, matching sizes — especially across different architectures (capacity gap → performance drop). Methods are sensitive to layer choice, alignment and transformation, so cross-method comparison is hard.

2.3 Similarity-based distillation

Transfers structural / relational knowledge — pairwise similarities between features or instances — rather than exact values.

L_SD = ℓ_similarity( φ(F_t^i, F_t^j) , φ(F_s^i, F_s^j) ) (5) i, j = two layers or two samples; φ computes the similarity; ℓ compares teacher vs. student similarity
  • Pioneers: FSP (Yim 2017) — "Flow of Solution Procedure": inner product between features of two layers in the teacher; squared L2 to student's. You 2017 (MTKD): relative dissimilarity between examples. Xie 2018: 8-neighbourhood similarity of logits. Zhang & Peng 2018: similarity among multiple teachers.
  • Instance/sample-level: RKD (Park 2019): distance- and angle-wise relations between samples. SPKD (Tung & Mori 2019): batch correlation via feature inner products. IRG (Liu 2019f): instance relationship graph. CIRKD (Yang 2022c): extends to global relations with a memory bank, pixel-to-pixel and pixel-to-region. Hu 2024b (GIRKD): stored features → fuller instance relations.
  • Feature/channel-level: IFVD (Wang 2020d): class prototypes; distill pixel-to-prototype distances. ICKD (Liu 2021c): channel correlation matrix (feature map × its transpose). Wang 2023d (CCD): minimize distance between channel-correlation maps.
  • Class-level: DIST / DistKD (Huang 2022a; 2025a): inter- and intra-class similarity. Feng 2021 (DSD): pairwise class similarity (logits × reshaped logits). Zhang 2022d (IDD): position info + inter-class distances for segmentation. AICSD (Mansourian 2025): intra-class distribution per class, pairwise KL between them (segmentation). IKD (Wang 2025a): knowledge sharing within a class.
  • Spatial: SKD (Liu 2019d): pool features into 9 regions; Wang 2023b multi-scale pixel-region; Wang 2023k (BCKD) adjacent-block correlations; Wang 2023a patch-level; Zhang 2024g (CRLD) logit similarity from local and global views.

Math of the main similarity losses standard form

FSP (Yim 2017) — flow matrix between two layers (inner product over spatial positions) G_{ij}(x) = (1 / (h·w)) · Σ_{s=1..h} Σ_{t=1..w} F¹_{i,s,t} · F²_{j,s,t} L = Σ_layer-pairs λ_k · ‖ G^T_k − G^S_k ‖²₂ RKD (Park 2019) — distance-wise and angle-wise relations over samples ψ_D(t_i, t_j) = ‖t_i − t_j‖₂ / μ (μ = mean pairwise distance, for scale invariance) ψ_A(t_i, t_j, t_k) = cos∠(t_i, t_j, t_k) = ⟨ e_ij , e_kj ⟩, e_ij = (t_i − t_j)/‖t_i − t_j‖ L_RKD = Σ ℓ_δ( ψ(t…), ψ(s…) ) (ℓ_δ = Huber loss) SPKD (Tung & Mori 2019) — batch similarity matrix G = Q · Qᵀ (Q ∈ R^{b×chw}, one row per sample); G̃ = row-wise L2-normalised G L = (1 / b²) · ‖ G̃_T − G̃_S ‖²_F ICKD (Liu 2021c) — inter-channel correlation A = F · Fᵀ ∈ R^{C×C} (F reshaped to C × HW) L = ‖ A_T − A_S ‖²_F (student A uses a projected feature) DIST (Huang 2022a) — correlation-based inter- and intra-class relations d(u, v) = 1 − ρ(u, v), ρ = Pearson correlation L_inter = (1/B) Σ_b d( Y^s_{b,:} , Y^t_{b,:} ) (relations among classes, per sample) L_intra = (1/C) Σ_c d( Y^s_{:,c} , Y^t_{:,c} ) (relations among samples, per class) CIRKD (Yang 2022c) — adds a memory bank M of past embeddings relations computed between the current pixel/region embedding and all bank entries: L = KL( softmax(sim(f_t, M)) ‖ softmax(sim(f_s, M)) ) (pixel-to-pixel and pixel-to-region)

Additional similarity details from the survey: Peng 2019 (CCKD) transfers correlation congruence; Xie 2018 distils the 8-neighbourhood similarity of logits (segmentation); Chen 2020b adds graph-style instance relations; Wang 2024g, Cheng 2024a and Hu 2024b extend instance-level relations (Hu 2024b stores features to reveal more complete instance relations); Wang 2020d (IFVD) builds a prototype per class and distils pixel-to-prototype distances; Zhang 2022d (IDD) adds position information; Mansourian 2025 (AICSD) builds an intra-class distribution per class and compares classes pairwise with KL; Wang 2025a (IKD) shares knowledge within a class for consistent predictions; Xin 2024 (NRKD) uses neighbour relations; Zhang 2024g (CRLD) uses local and global logit similarity; Wang 2023a distils patch-level similarity.

Online methods

  • Early: Zhu 2018 and Guo 2020b — ensemble of multi-branch logits forms an on-the-fly teacher. Wu & Gong 2021 — mean of previous predictions as auxiliary guide. Li 2021b — branches of varying depth; each Hourglass net mimics the final heatmap and the fused heatmap of all branches (pose). Yang 2023b — mutual contrastive learning.
  • Recent: Rao 2023 (reduce sharpness/variance gap in logit space); Zhang 2023h (flatter loss minima for generalization/stability); Jacob 2023 (multi-task nets using single-task networks as teachers, adaptive feature distillation + online task weighting); Ypsilantis 2024 (multi-domain, one teacher per domain).

Self-distillation methods

  • Born-Again Networks (Furlanello 2018): first train on labels; then a new identical model with a different random seed is trained under the former's guidance. Lan 2019: labels + features of the model-so-far. Kim 2021a: linear combo of hard targets and the previous epoch's predictions. Zhang 2019b: split the model into sections, transfer deeper → shallower. Zhang 2021c: multiple shallow classifiers at different depths learn from the deepest.
  • Theory: Mobahi 2020 — first theoretical analysis; Zhang & Sabuncu 2020 — link between self-distillation and label smoothing.
  • Recent flavours: deeper layers → shallower (Liang 2024; Qin 2025); previous epochs as teacher (Liu 2024e; Li 2024i; Lin 2024b); iterative pruning to create a student (Chen 2024a; Zheng 2024b); MSKD for multi-label learning (Wang 2025c); graph + self-distillation (Wu 2024a; Zhang 2023b); data augmentation + deep contrastive learning + self-distillation on different views of one sample (Xiao 2023b).

4. Distillation algorithms

4.1 Attention-based distillation

Attention reflects neuron selectivity; attention maps highlight crucial regions so the student mimics what matters.

  • AT (Zagoruyko & Komodakis 2016): first; aggregate channels → minimize loss between attention maps.
  • Feng 2021: residual attention maps from two layers. An 2022: self-attention module adaptively aggregating context. Yuan 2024b: self-attention to soften logits.
  • CAM / Grad-CAM based transfer of high-attention regions: Bavandpour & Kasaei 2020; Cho & Kang 2022; Guo 2023d (CAT-KD).
  • Karine 2024: channel + position self-attention. Gou 2023: different attention types at different representation levels. Guo 2024b: student features as queries, teacher as keys/values; sparse attention via random deactivation re-weights each teacher–student feature distance to avoid negative transfer.
  • Recent strong: DiffKD (diffusion denoising to emphasise important areas); AttnFD (CBAM; notably helps small objects in semantic segmentation).

4.2 Adversarial distillation

Inspired by the GAN min-max game (Goodfellow 2014). Three uses:

  1. Generator synthesizes training data (data-free KD).
  2. Discriminator distinguishes teacher vs. student outputs → refined guidance.
  3. Adversarial training to reduce teacher–student discrepancy on perturbed (harder) samples → robustness.

4.2.1 Adversarial data-free KD

Used when original data is unavailable (privacy, transmission, legal constraints; Fang 2019). Adversarial samples are synthesized to mimic or augment the dataset. Often the pretrained teacher and its internal statistics act as the discriminator guiding the generator (Chen 2019a; Choi 2020; Wang 2024o; Ye 2020; Zhao 2022b; Micaelli & Storkey 2019). Others: Do 2022 — use an EMA of the student as a more reliable learning source under distribution shift; Patel 2023 — meta-learning that balances knowledge acquisition and retention; Zhou 2024d — self-adversarial strategies for robustness; Liao 2024 — imbalanced datasets (teacher trained on skewed classes) with class-aware strategies.

4.2.2 Discriminator in the loop

  • Discriminator aligns logits (Xu 2017; Liu 2019a; Ghorbani 2020; Croitoru 2024) or intermediate features (Liu 2020a; Wang 2020a; Chung 2020; Belagiannis 2018; etc.). Three-player frameworks (Wang 2018b; 2019).
  • GAN compression: distil heavy generators into efficient ones (Aguinaldo 2019; Wang 2020c; Li 2020a; Zhang 2022a).
  • Diffusion models: discriminators used to reduce inference steps (Sauer 2024; Liu 2024a; Kong 2024; Lin 2024c). Score-based: adversarial score distillation (Wei 2024a), variational score distillation (Wan 2024).

4.2.3 Adversarial robust KD

Plain KD gives no inherent robustness. ARD (Goldblum 2020) combines adversarial training with distillation: make the student's predictions on perturbed inputs match the teacher's predictions on clean inputs.

4.3 Multi-teacher distillation

Student learns from several teachers → diverse knowledge, better generalization. Origin: Hinton 2015 (average teachers' logits as soft labels).

  • Basic variants: You 2017 — voting; Sau & Balasubramanian 2016 — noise-perturbed logits treated as distinct teachers (regularizer); TeKAP (Hossain 2025) — synthetic multiple teachers by perturbing a single teacher; Fukuda 2017 — random teacher per step; Papernot 2016a — teachers on data subsets + voting; Iordache 2025 — teachers on distinct datasets; Furlanello 2018 — sequential: each step's student becomes the next teacher.
  • Adaptive aggregation (capacity gap per teacher): Lin 2024d (adaptive temperature + diverse aggregation); Cheng 2024b (separate vanilla KD loss; weights from entropy of each teacher's predictions); Wang & Xu 2024 (data-relation knowledge for weights); Zhang 2023a (meta-network weights).
  • Other aggregation: Chen 2019b (one teacher for intricate features, another for general decision features); Park & Kwak 2020, Asif 2020 (extra student branches); Cao 2023 (progressive).
  • Knowledge Amalgamation (KA): merge already-trained public networks (Shen 2019a/b; Vongkulbhisal 2019; Luo 2020; Amirkhani 2021; Xu 2022b; Zhang 2023c; Li 2024b — uncertainty suppression). Teachers may differ by dataset or task. Vongkulbhisal 2019: unified classifier from pre-trained classifiers + unlabeled generic data.
Challenges effective aggregation, deciding each teacher's contribution, complexity/computational cost.

4.4 Cross-modal distillation (CMKD)

Use a well-annotated (teacher/auxiliary) modality to improve a less-annotated (student/main) modality; the student works even when the auxiliary modality is absent at inference. Origin: Gupta 2016 (RGB → depth/optical flow).

  • Early pattern: two parallel streams at training, a single stream at inference. Garcia 2018/2019: RGB+depth → RGB (hallucinate depth). Thoker & Gall 2019: two students mimic each other. Wang 2021d (EvDistill): bidirectional events ↔ frames. Liu 2021g: 2D → 3D features.
  • LiDAR/Radar → RGB: Hong 2022b (CMKD), Zhou 2023b (UniDistill), Wang 2023p (DistillBEV), Klingner 2023 (X3KD); Zhao 2024b (CRKD; Radar instead of LiDAR); Zhang 2024c (Radocc; depth + semantic features instead of voxels). Zhang 2023i: shared thermal+RGB student with two heavier teachers. Lee 2023b: optical flow ↔ RGB, task-specific decomposition.
  • Text ↔ image/audio-video: Andonian 2022 (soft alignment); Wang 2024j (CM-MaskSD: text features guide masked image prediction); Sarkar & Etemad 2024 (XKD: mask audio + video, reconstruct).

4.5 Graph-based distillation

Graphs (nodes = features/instances; edges = dependencies) capture higher-order, local and global relations beyond pairwise similarity (Zhang & Peng 2018; Chen 2020b; Park 2019; Tung & Mori 2019; Peng 2019; Liu 2019f; Lee & Song 2019; Passalis 2020; Zhou 2021a; etc.). Graphs can also regulate knowledge flow among multiple teachers (Luo 2018; Minami 2020).

GNN distillation (Tian 2023a) has two objectives:

  1. Performance improvement of the original model (Zhang 2020a; 2021e; Chen 2020e; Rezayi 2021; Guo 2022/2023c; Huo 2023; Zhu 2024b; Yu 2022a).
  2. Compression: big GNN → compact GNN (Dong 2023c; Yang 2022b; He 2022; Deng & Zhang 2021) or even an MLP (Yang 2021; Zhang 2021d; Zheng 2021; Tian 2022a) for real-time use.

Transferred information: logits, feature representations, and the graph structure (connectivity) itself.

4.7 Contrastive distillation new category

Contrastive learning pulls positives together and pushes negatives apart (Hadsell 2006). In KD: anchor = student embedding, positive = teacher embedding of the same input, negatives = unrelated embeddings (teacher, student, or input-data based; Fig. 6).

6. Applications

6.1 Distillation in large language models

Goal: compress LLMs into Small Language Models (SLMs). Fig. 8 taxonomy:

White-boxTeacher logits and intermediate outputs accessible → student uses internal info (logits, hidden states, attention scores).
Subtypes: BERT-based and decoder-based.
Black-boxOnly teacher predictions available (closed-source GPT-4, Gemini).
Subtypes: In-context learning (ICL), Chain-of-thought (CoT), Instruction following (IF).

White-box: BERT-based

  • DistilBERT (Sanh 2019): student initialized from teacher; soft-probability matching during pre-training.
  • MiniLM (Wang 2020b): distil the self-attention module of the teacher's last transformer layer.
  • MobileBERT (Sun 2020): trains a special teacher first, then feature + attention transfer.
  • TinyBERT (Jiao 2019): both pre-training and task-specific distillation; logits, attention matrices, hidden states, and embedding-layer output.
  • PKD (Sun 2019): two incremental-distillation strategies. Turc 2019 (PD, pre-trained distillation). XtremeDistil / XtremeDistilTransformers (Mukherjee & Awadallah 2020; Mukherjee 2021): architecture-agnostic / task-agnostic. MetaDistill (Zhou 2021b): meta-learning feedback.

White-box: decoder-based / recent

  • GKD (Agarwal 2023/2024): train student on its self-generated outputs. PC-LoRA (Hwang 2024): progressive compression + fine-tuning concurrently. MiniLLM (Gu 2024): use reverse KL so the student does not overestimate low-probability regions of the teacher distribution. TED (Liang 2023a). CKA-based (Dasgupta & Cohn 2025): match hidden states of different dimensionality → smaller students, higher compression. Gong 2025: align feature dynamics.
  • Industry pre-training-stage KD: Liu 2023b (data-free quantization-aware training — a quantized teacher version trained as student); NVIDIA pruning-aware distillation (Muralidharan 2024: prune teacher's neurons then post-train with a small portion of data via KD; Sreenivas 2024: adds a teacher-correction step); Gemma 3 (logit sampling) and Qwen3 (weak-to-strong distillation).

Black-box

  • ICL (introduced in GPT-3): teacher gets task description + few examples + query; student mimics the response. Huang 2022e (ILD): combine ICL objective with LM objective; AICD (Liu 2024): jointly distil ICL + reasoning (likelihood of all rationales in context); LLM-R (Wang 2023h): iteratively train dense retrievers that find high-quality in-context examples.
  • Instruction following: teacher generates task instructions; student fine-tuned. Self-Instruct (generate instruction/input/output → filter → fine-tune); UniversalNER (mission-focused instruction tuning); Lion (adversarial generation of hard instructions); LaMini-LM (2.58M instructions).
  • Chain-of-thought (most popular): teacher produces rationales + predictions; student learns to produce similar reasoning (analogous to feature learning). First: Li 2022e. Magister 2022: model-size vs dataset-size trade-off, reasoning transferable to SLMs. Fine-tune-CoT (Ho 2022). Distilling step-by-step (Hsieh 2023): multi-task learning with rationales. MCC-KD (Chen 2023a): multiple rationales per sample, enforcing consistency and diversity. TDG (Li 2024g): uses both positive and negative (incorrect-label) reasoning. RevThink (Chen 2024c): backward questions + reasoning, train on forward + backward → better generalization. Others: SCOTT, CSoTD, DOCTOR, PaD, MiniPLM.
White vs. black summary White-box: more effective (internal access) but costly and most strong LLMs are closed. Black-box: works on any model but only sees teacher-generated data → may be less general. Suggested strategy: first black-box distil closed-source → open-source model, then apply white-box distillation from that open model.

Math for LLM distillation standard form

TinyBERT (layer-wise): L = L_emb + Σ_{m=1..M} ( L_hid^(m) + L_attn^(m) ) + L_pred L_emb = MSE( E_S W_e , E_T ) embedding layer L_hid = MSE( H_S W_h , H_T ) hidden states L_attn = (1/h) Σ_{i=1..h} MSE( A_i^S , A_i^T ) attention matrices L_pred = − softmax(z_T/τ) · log softmax(z_S/τ) soft cross-entropy on logits DistilBERT: L = α·L_CE(soft) + β·L_MLM + γ·L_cos( h_S , h_T ) (soft-label + masked-LM + cosine embedding) MiniLM (last layer): L_attn = KL( A_T^last ‖ A_S^last ) ; L_val = KL( VR_T ‖ VR_S ) , VR = softmax( V Vᵀ / √d ) (value-relation) MiniLLM (reverse KL): L = KL( q_θ ‖ p ) = E_{y ~ q_θ(·|x)} [ log q_θ(y|x) − log p(y|x) ] → mode-seeking: student avoids putting mass where the teacher has low probability (forward KL(p ‖ q) is mean-seeking → student over-estimates low-probability regions) GKD (on-policy): L = E_x E_{y ~ p_S(·|x)} [ D( p_T ‖ p_S )( y | x ) ] , D = forward KL, reverse KL or generalised JSD (train on the student's own samples to remove train/inference distribution mismatch) Chain-of-thought distillation: teacher outputs rationale r and answer a; L = − log p_S( r , a | x ) Step-by-step multi-task (Hsieh 2023): L = L_label( x → a ) + λ · L_rationale( x → r ) Backward reasoning (RevThink): L = L_forward( x → r , a ) + L_backward-question( x,a → x̃ ) + L_backward-reasoning( x̃ → r̃ ) Instruction following (Self-Instruct): data D = { (instruction, input, output) } generated by the teacher, filtered; L = − Σ log p_S( output | instruction, input ) In-context distillation: L = L_LM + λ·L_ICL , L_ICL = − log p_S( y_query | examples , x_query ) (ILD, Huang 2022e) Compression rate (Table 16): rate = #params_teacher / #params_student ; "Comparison with teacher" = score_student / score_teacher × 100%

6.3 Distillation in self-supervised learning

Self-distillation underpins the most prominent SSL methods.

  • SimCLR (Chen 2020c): shared network, two augmentations, align embeddings. MoCo (He 2020; Chen 2020d) and BYOL (Grill 2020): same network, but student trained by backprop and teacher = EMA of student. SwAV (Caron 2020): prototypes; student predicts same cluster code as the teacher for augmentations of the same image. DINO / DINOv2: global + local crops through teacher (EMA) and student, representations forced to match — "local-to-global" suits segmentation.
  • More complex: BINGO (Xu 2021; pretrained SSL groups images into bags, inter- and intra-sample losses); Wang 2023l (two SSL teachers — image & video — with masked modeling; spatial features from image teacher, spatio-temporal from video teacher); Song 2023a (parallel SSL + consistency losses); Gao 2022c (known KD within SSL using a pretrained SSL model).
  • Distilling a pretrained SSL model into a small one: Abbasi Koohpayegani 2020 (CompRess; similarity-based; gap with supervised shrinks as model grows; distilled model can outperform the supervised counterpart); SEED (Fang 2021; queue of anchors, match student–anchor distances to teacher–anchor distances); Navaneet 2022 (effect of an MLP after student representations); Tejankar 2021; Dadashzadeh 2022 (auxiliary pre-training phase for small unlabeled datasets).

Math for self-distillation in SSL standard form

SimCLR (NT-Xent): ℓ_{i,j} = − log [ exp( sim(z_i,z_j)/τ ) / Σ_{k≠i} exp( sim(z_i,z_k)/τ ) ] (two augmentations of one image) BYOL (EMA teacher): θ_T ← m·θ_T + (1 − m)·θ_S L = ‖ q(z_S)/‖q(z_S)‖ − sg(z_T)/‖z_T‖ ‖² = 2 − 2·cos( q(z_S), sg(z_T) ) (sg = stop-gradient; q = predictor) MoCo: InfoNCE against a queue of teacher-encoded keys; key encoder = EMA of the query encoder SwAV: L = ℓ( z_1 , q_2 ) + ℓ( z_2 , q_1 ) , q = cluster code (Sinkhorn-Knopp assignment to prototypes); student predicts the teacher's code DINO: p_T = softmax( ( g_T(x_global) − c ) / τ_T ) (centering c, sharp τ_T) p_S = softmax( g_S(x) / τ_S ) L = Σ_{x ∈ global views} Σ_{x' ∈ all views, x' ≠ x} − p_T(x) · log p_S(x') (local-to-global correspondence) θ_T ← m·θ_T + (1 − m)·θ_S SEED (Fang 2021): with a queue of anchors A: L = − Σ_a softmax_a( sim(z_T, a)/τ_T ) · log softmax_a( sim(z_S, a)/τ_S ) (student matches the teacher's similarity distribution over anchors; similarity-based SSL distillation)

Survey details. The paper states that self-distillation is "the basis of the most prominent works in SSL, especially in vision". SimCLR is described as a shared network receiving two augmentations; MoCo/BYOL use the same network with a different training procedure (student by backprop, teacher = EMA); SwAV assigns codes via prototypes; DINO / DINOv2 pass global and local patches through teacher and student, and the local-to-global design suits segmentation. More complex: BINGO (bag of samples from a pretrained SSL model; inter- and intra-sample losses per epoch), Wang 2023l (image teacher + video teacher with masked modelling), Song 2023a (parallel SSL with consistency losses), Gao 2022c, Gu 2021a, Misra & Maaten 2020, Xu 2021. Small-model SSL: the gap between supervised and self-supervised nets shrinks with model size (Abbasi Koohpayegani 2020); Navaneet 2022 studies an MLP layer after the student's representation; Tejankar 2021; Dadashzadeh 2022 (auxiliary pre-training phase for small unlabeled data).

Math for diffusion distillation standard form

Forward process: x_t = √ᾱ_t · x_0 + √(1 − ᾱ_t) · ε , ε ~ N(0, I) teacher reverse step: x_{t−1} = Φ_T( x_t, t ) Progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho 2022): student takes ONE step to match TWO teacher steps x̃ = Φ_T( Φ_T( x_t, t ), t − 1 ) L = ‖ Φ_S( x_t, t ) − x̃ ‖² then student → new teacher; repeat (N → N/2 → N/4 …) Distribution matching (Yin 2024c): one-step generator G_θ ; minimise KL( p_fake ‖ p_real ) with gradient ∇_θ KL ≈ − E_z [ ( s_real( x_t ) − s_fake( x_t ) ) · ∂G_θ(z)/∂θ ] (s = score functions of the real / fake distributions) Consistency models (Song 2023c): f_θ( x_t, t ) → x_0 for every t on the same trajectory; f_θ( x_ε, ε ) = x_ε (boundary condition) consistency-distillation loss: L = d( f_θ( x_{t_{n+1}}, t_{n+1} ) , f_{θ⁻}( x̂_{t_n}, t_n ) ) θ⁻ = EMA(θ), x̂_{t_n} = one ODE step with the teacher Score distillation (DreamFusion / ProlificDreamer): ∇_φ L_SDS = E_{t,ε}[ w(t) · ( ε̂_ψ( x_t ; y, t ) − ε ) · ∂x/∂φ ] (φ = parameters of the 3D representation, ε̂_ψ = frozen 2D diffusion model's noise prediction, y = text prompt) Feature-level: L = ‖ F_S( x_t , t ) − F_T( x_t , t ) ‖² + denoising/guidance variants (Huang 2023b; Hsiao 2024; Feng 2024b; Zhang 2024b)

Feature-level (Huang 2023b; Hsiao 2024; Feng 2024b; Zhang 2024b), sampling process (Salimans & Ho 2022; Yin 2024c; Zhou 2024e), adversarial & data-free (Sauer 2024; Mekonnen 2024; EM distillation, Xie 2024b — bootstraps between successive denoising steps, no big synthetic set), consistency models (Song 2023c; Song & Dhariwal 2023; Geng 2024; latent variants Luo 2023a, Xie 2024a; Lu & Song 2024 for stability/scalability), cross-modal/universal (ProlificDreamer, DreamFusion, Diff-Instruct). Motivation: diffusion needs hundreds to thousands of denoising steps; distilled models target the same quality with far fewer steps and much less inference time/compute.

8.3 Quantitative comparison

See §7: hyperparameters + teacher weights dominate, so authors avoid direct "which is better" claims, but highlight strengths per category in each section and report both same- and different-architecture teacher/student pairs.

8.4 Practical and ethical aspects

  • Privacy: KD to reduce memorization in text-to-image models (Fernandez 2023), reduce LLM leakage of sensitive info (Flemings & Annavaram 2024), train a large teacher without exposing edge-device data (Shao 2024a). But DFKD does not inherently guarantee privacy (Zhao & Zhang 2025) and may add vulnerabilities in LLMs (Cui 2025).
  • Fairness: KD can steer the final student toward more equitable behaviour (Chai 2022).
  • Energy / sustainability: cheaper students → lower energy use (Yuan 2024c).
  • Legal/IP: using commercial models as teachers may violate copyright/terms — must be considered in research and deployment.

8.5 Challenges

  1. Knowledge extraction. Choosing the source: logits fail without labels and can't serve foundation-model embeddings; closed-source LLMs hide logits/features → black-box only. Methods often combine sources.
  2. Choosing the scheme. Offline when a big pretrained model exists; online otherwise; self-distillation for SSL. Multi-teacher ("mixture of experts") needs more memory/GPUs. For FMs/LLMs, co-training teacher and student is infeasible → offline dominates.
  3. Capacity gap. A bigger teacher is not always better (receptive fields differ; Cho & Hariharan 2019; Guo 2020a; Huang 2022a). Worse for FMs; black-box LLMs can't transfer intermediate layers. Remedy: methods that make teacher knowledge more digestible.
  4. Architectural difference. Different architectures → degradation. Logit KD remains OK across architectures; feature/similarity KD must select semantically matching layers (different inductive biases). In black-box LLM distillation, CoT sidesteps layer mimicry by giving reasoning steps — but generating good rationales and bridging the capacity gap remain hard.

8.6 Future directions

  • Feature-based distillation: trending to even larger gains; simple transformations on features can reach SOTA (Liu 2023c; 2024g); distilling embeddings of FMs like CLIP is growing.
  • Adaptive distillation: how to distil informative knowledge while ignoring unused/wrong knowledge is largely unexplored (Hsu 2022; Ojha 2023); analogous to human teaching (teaching assistant, decaying teacher influence, sample-importance weighting).
  • Distillation from foundation models: CLIP text + image embeddings for weakly supervised segmentation; open-vocabulary tasks.
  • LLM distillation: over-parameterized, sparse weights; most strong LLMs closed → black-box importance; CoT distillation to transfer reasoning; KD in pre-training stage in industry (create smaller versions of a big LLM with quantization/pruning + a small fraction of original data; Qwen3, Gemma 3).
